# Safety-First Driver Learning Path

Status: product and curriculum baseline. This document defines what the platform must teach and assess; it does not certify driving competence or replace an approved driving school, qualified instructor, or official licensing assessment.

## Product promise

The platform prepares a learner to understand the road, make safer decisions, recognise developing hazards, protect vulnerable road users, and understand the Saudi licensing journey. Passing a knowledge quiz is not the product outcome. The intended outcome is safer, more responsible road use.

The Saudi track is primary. Legal requirements and licensing procedures must be tied to current Saudi primary sources. General safety advice must be labelled as guidance, not presented as Saudi law. International examples are comparative only and must never silently replace Saudi rules.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the learning journey, a learner should be able to:

1. Explain the meaning and required response for the Saudi traffic signs, signals, road markings, and temporary controls included in the verified catalogue.
2. Interpret road layouts and make safe decisions at junctions, roundabouts, merges, crossings, and lane changes.
3. Demonstrate knowledge of vehicle controls, pre-drive checks, occupant protection, and basic vehicle-readiness warnings.
4. Identify hazards early, adapt speed and following distance to conditions, and choose a safe response.
5. Share the road responsibly with pedestrians, children, older people, people with disabilities, cyclists, motorcyclists, public transport, and heavy vehicles.
6. Explain how darkness, rain, fog, dust, heat, road works, and poor visibility affect risk and driving decisions.
7. Describe safe actions during a breakdown, tyre failure, collision, or other roadside emergency, including when and how to seek help.
8. Recognise the risks of distraction, fatigue, impairment, aggression, and unsafe vehicle loading.
9. Understand the difference between learning-platform progress, supervised practical competence, and official licensing decisions.

## Curriculum map

### 1. Road language and traffic-control devices

- Warning signs: identify the hazard, anticipate where it may occur, and choose an appropriate response.
- Regulatory signs: restrictions, prohibitions, mandatory movements, speed controls, stopping/parking controls, and priority controls.
- Guide and information signs: route, destination, lane, service, and facility information.
- Traffic signals: vehicle and pedestrian signals, signal sequencing where officially documented, and safe response to unusual or failed signals.
- Road markings and delineation: lane boundaries, edge lines, stop lines, crossing markings, arrows, hatching, and reflective markers.
- Temporary traffic control: work-zone signs, diversions, cones, barriers, channelising devices, and authorised directions.
- Supplementary plates and sign combinations: read the complete instruction rather than a symbol in isolation.

Required practice: image recognition, sign-in-context interpretation, mixed device tests, and explanation of the safety purpose. Each published catalogue entry needs a verified Saudi source, usage-rights record for its visual, editorial review, and revision history.

### 2. Road positioning and manoeuvres

- Moving off and stopping; steering and speed control.
- Lane selection, lane discipline, mirrors, indicators, and blind-spot checks.
- Safe lane changes and merging.
- Junction approach, observation, priority, and turning.
- Roundabouts and multi-lane circulation.
- Overtaking and being overtaken.
- Reversing, parking, hill starts, and low-speed manoeuvres.
- Expressways, entrances, exits, service roads, and lane closures.

Required practice: animated or illustrated original scenarios, decision sequencing, and mistake explanations. The app must not imply that a quiz proves the learner can physically perform a manoeuvre.

### 3. Defensive driving and hazard perception

- Observation routines and scanning ahead, to the sides, and behind.
- Identifying clues that a hazard may develop.
- Reaction time, stopping distance, and safe following distance.
- Speed choice based on visibility, road surface, traffic, load, and available space.
- Escape space, avoiding sudden manoeuvres, and managing tailgating.
- Intersections, parked vehicles, school zones, buses, and hidden hazards.
- Safe decisions when another road user behaves unpredictably.

Required practice: original still-image and video-style hazard scenarios, hazard identification, timing/sequence decisions, and a reasoned explanation. Do not copy proprietary hazard-test clips or question banks.

### 4. Sharing the road and vulnerable road users

- Pedestrians, marked and unmarked crossing situations, and pedestrian desire lines.
- Children near schools, buses, and parked vehicles.
- Older people and people with disabilities or reduced mobility.
- Wheelchair users and accessible crossings.
- Cyclists and micromobility users.
- Motorcycles and their visibility/space needs.
- Public transport, school buses, and passenger loading areas.
- Heavy vehicles, large turning areas, and blind spots.
- Emergency vehicles and safe yielding without creating a new hazard.

Required practice: perspective-taking scenarios that ask what each road user can see, what they may do next, and how the learner can reduce risk.

### 5. Weather, visibility, and road environments

- Night driving, glare, and headlight use.
- Rain, standing water, reduced grip, and spray.
- Fog, dust, sand, and reduced visibility.
- Heat, tyre condition, vehicle overheating, and long journeys.
- Slopes, curves, tunnels, bridges, and open highways.
- Road works, diversions, temporary surfaces, and debris.
- Rural/desert roads, animals, and remote-road preparation where relevant.

Required practice: compare the same situation under different conditions and select a safe speed, gap, visibility strategy, or decision to stop/avoid travel.

### 6. Vehicle readiness and occupant protection

- Pre-drive walk-around and basic readiness checks.
- Tyres, lights, mirrors, windows, wipers, brakes, and warning indicators.
- Seat belts, head restraints, child restraints, and safe passenger seating.
- Load security and visibility.
- Dashboard warnings: distinguish stop-now warnings from items needing prompt inspection using verified vehicle-manufacturer guidance.
- Maintenance awareness and the limits of driver self-inspection.

Required practice: labelled original diagrams, checklists, and scenario questions. Avoid giving model-specific mechanical instructions without a source.

### 7. Breakdowns, collisions, and first response

- Moving to a safer location where possible.
- Warning other road users without placing oneself in danger.
- Breakdown and tyre-failure response.
- Collision scene safety, emergency contacts, and reporting routes verified for Saudi Arabia.
- Basic first-aid awareness: protect life, call trained responders, and avoid actions beyond one's training.
- Fire, smoke, and other urgent vehicle hazards.
- Supporting vulnerable or injured people while waiting for responders.

Required practice: ordered response scenarios. Emergency contact numbers and reporting procedures must be verified against current official Saudi sources before publication.

### 8. Driver fitness, conduct, and responsibility

- Distraction and mobile-phone risk.
- Fatigue, sleep, long journeys, and stopping to rest.
- Alcohol, drugs, medicines, and impairment.
- Aggression, pressure, road rage, and emotional state.
- Speeding, unsafe gaps, and risky competition.
- Legal duties, penalties, and reporting obligations only when supported by current Saudi legal sources.
- Respectful conduct and shared responsibility for road safety.

Required practice: decision scenarios and reflection prompts. Separate legal consequences from general safety recommendations.

### 9. Saudi licensing journey and supervised practice

- Explain the verified Saudi licensing steps, eligibility, documents, training, examinations, and official service channels.
- Distinguish learner education from formal training and official assessment.
- Provide a supervised-practice log for skills to discuss with a qualified instructor.
- Clearly state that platform completion does not issue a licence or establish legal driving entitlement.

All procedural information must show its official source, last-verified date, and a review status. Remove or flag stale guidance when official requirements change.

## Teaching pattern for every topic

Each learning unit follows this sequence:

1. **Notice:** introduce the road feature, rule, or hazard.
2. **Understand:** explain what it means and why it matters.
3. **Decide:** present a realistic situation and ask what the learner should do.
4. **Explain:** show why the safer answer is appropriate and why alternatives are unsafe or incomplete.
5. **Retrieve:** revisit the concept later using a different question or context.
6. **Apply:** where a physical skill is involved, direct the learner to supervised practice with a qualified instructor.

Use plain Arabic first, with verified English terminology as a supporting label. Keep Saudi legal terms distinct from international terms when their meanings differ.

## Assessment and progression

Assessment is competency-based, not lesson-completion-based:

- Diagnostic baseline across all curriculum domains.
- Short topic checks immediately after learning.
- Fresh scenario questions that test application, not memorised wording.
- Hazard-perception exercises that assess noticing and prioritisation.
- Spaced review of missed concepts.
- Mixed and timed practice tests only after broad topic coverage.
- A safety-critical misconception check before the learner can receive an internal readiness summary.

The platform should track mastery separately for: signs/devices, rules/priority, manoeuvres knowledge, hazard perception, vulnerable road users, adverse conditions, vehicle readiness, emergency response, and driver fitness.

A learner must not receive a high overall readiness label while failing a critical safety domain. Thresholds must be set after question-quality review and pilot data; do not invent a passing threshold or describe the result as an official score or pass probability.

## Question and scenario quality requirements

Every question/scenario must include:

- Stable ID, Arabic wording, optional verified English wording, and content version.
- Jurisdiction (Saudi Arabia for the primary track), vehicle class, and applicable learner level.
- Curriculum domain, subtopic, and measurable learning objective.
- Format: single choice, multiple select, image interpretation, sequence, or hazard scenario.
- Correct answer and explanation.
- Rationale for each distractor, including the misconception or risk it represents.
- Source references and the exact fact supported by each source.
- Evidence type: binding Saudi rule, official Saudi guidance, general safety principle, or comparative international material.
- Rights/licence information for all images, video, and adapted material.
- Review status, reviewer, review date, and change history.

Do not publish questions that rely on unsourced legal claims, ambiguous road situations, misleading images, or copied proprietary test content.

## Editorial and release gates

1. **Draft:** authoring only; hidden from learners.
2. **Source checked:** source, date/version, jurisdiction, and rights reviewed.
3. **Safety reviewed:** content checked for misleading or unsafe instruction.
4. **Traffic/legal reviewed:** required for Saudi legal or procedural claims by a suitably qualified reviewer.
5. **Published:** all required checks complete.
6. **Retired:** outdated or superseded content retained for audit but not served to learners.

High-risk safety and legal content should receive independent second-person review before publication. A source link alone is not proof that the lesson's interpretation is correct.

## Definition of a responsible readiness report

The report may say which topics the learner has practised, where their knowledge is strong or weak, and what to revise next. It must also state:

- It is an educational progress indicator, not an official test result.
- It cannot verify actual vehicle-control skills or road behaviour.
- It does not replace approved driver training, supervised practice, or official testing.
- A learner who has not practised a domain must not be shown as competent in it.

## Build sequence

1. Define the structured content model and editorial workflow.
2. Add curriculum domains, objectives, sources, and review states to the database design.
3. Build the learner-facing learning-path and topic catalogue.
4. Build question types, scenario delivery, secure server-side grading, and per-domain mastery.
5. Add original hazard-perception media and accessible alternatives.
6. Add supervised-practice logs and responsible readiness reporting.
7. Pilot with qualified Saudi driving/traffic-safety reviewers and learners before public release.

Database migrations and production data changes require a separate reviewed implementation step. Existing user, lesson, question, and attempt data must be preserved.
