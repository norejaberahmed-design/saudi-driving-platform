# International Driver-Theory Test Research and Saudi Learning Design

Research snapshot: 16 November 2026.
Scope of this first evidence pass: official international frameworks and primary government sources from the United Kingdom, European Union, Singapore, New South Wales (Australia), California (United States), and the United Nations Economic Commission for Europe. This is a structured first tranche, not a claim that every country's current test has already been exhaustively audited.

## Executive finding

The strongest common foundation across the reviewed systems is not memorising isolated question banks. It is mastery of:
- road signs, markings, traffic signals and right-of-way;
- speed, lane discipline, junctions, roundabouts, overtaking, stopping and parking;
- observation, attention, judgement, hazard anticipation and safe decisions;
- vulnerable road users and sharing the road;
- safe distance, braking, weather, visibility, night driving and road conditions;
- vehicle condition, controls, occupant protection and emergency response;
- impairment, fatigue, distraction and driver fitness.

The platform should teach the common safety concepts once, then show jurisdiction-specific rule cards. Saudi requirements are the primary exam-preparation track. International modules are comparative learning only unless a separate country track is deliberately selected.

## Official evidence reviewed

### United Kingdom — DVSA / GOV.UK
- Theory preparation is based on the Highway Code, Know Your Traffic Signs and Driving: The Essential Skills.
- The car theory test includes multiple-choice and hazard-perception preparation.
- Hazard perception uses video clips of everyday road scenes and developing hazards; scoring rewards timely recognition rather than simple recall.
- Sources:
  - https://www.gov.uk/theory-test/revision-and-practice
  - https://www.gov.uk/theory-test/hazard-perception-test

### European Union — minimum knowledge and test framework
The EU framework specifies both a theory test and a practical skills/behaviour test. The common theory syllabus includes road rules (signs, markings, signals, right of way, speed), driver alertness and attitude, hazard perception and judgement, effects of alcohol/drugs/medicines/fatigue, safe distance and braking, weather/road risk, road types and tunnels, and other road users. Individual member states determine the exact question form and national implementation.
- Source:
  - https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32025L2205

### Singapore — Traffic Police official handbooks
The official Basic Theory of Driving handbook organises examinable content into signs and signals, traffic rules and regulations, and related road-user controls. Its listed areas include mandatory, prohibitory, warning, regulatory and information signs; pedestrian/tunnel/cycle/directional/facility signs; vehicle and road markings; arrows; traffic lights; police and hand signals; lane discipline; overtaking; speed; give way; roundabouts; expressways; U-turns; stopping/parking; seat belts; lights; alcohol.
The Final Theory handbook extends into vehicle controls, warning indicators, pre-drive checks, moving off/stopping, bends, lane changes, junctions, roundabouts, overtaking, slopes and reversing.
- Sources:
  - https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Knowledge-Hub/Traffic/BT-ENG-24126.pdf
  - https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Advisories/FTTEN24.pdf
- Copyright note: the handbooks explicitly restrict reproduction. Use them for topic analysis and citation; do not copy their questions, diagrams or text into the product without permission.

### New South Wales — Transport for NSW / Service NSW
- The Road User Handbook covers licensing, safe driving, sharing roads, lanes/lines/markings, parking, warnings/hazards, registration and penalties.
- The DKT tests road rules and safe driving behaviour. The official online learning route is a structured interactive course with modules, quizzes and a final test.
- The state also separates the knowledge test, hazard perception test and practical driving test in its licensing pathway.
- Published DKT question resources are explicitly limited to the in-person test; do not assume they apply to the online course.
- Sources:
  - https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/driver-and-rider-licences/publications
  - https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/driver-and-rider-licences/driver/tests/dkt
  - https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/driver-and-rider-licences/driver/tests

### California — California DMV
- DMV directs learners to its Driver's Handbook and sample tests for the relevant licence class.
- Knowledge tests establish understanding of road rules and safety; practical drive tests assess driving performance.
- Separate materials exist for cars, motorcycles and commercial drivers.
- Sources:
  - https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/driver-licenses-identification-cards/preparing-for-knowledge-and-drive-tests/
  - https://qr.dmv.ca.gov/portal/driver-handbooks/

### International harmonisation — UNECE / United Nations
- The 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic and Convention on Road Signs and Signals provide an international framework for participating countries.
- The conventions do not make every country's signs, exceptions, penalties, lane rules or examination identical. National legislation and implementation remain essential.
- Sources:
  - https://unece.org/road-traffic-and-road-signs-and-signals-agreements-and-conventions
  - https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetailsIII.aspx?chapter=11&mtdsg_no=XI-B-19&src=IND

## Learning architecture to implement

### Track A — Saudi licence preparation (primary)
All examinable legal statements, sign meanings, speed/priority rules and licensing procedures must be sourced to current Saudi primary material. The UI labels this track clearly as Saudi-specific.

### Track B — Universal road-safety foundations
Teach shared safety principles that transfer across countries:
- observation and scanning;
- anticipation and hazard response;
- safe following distance and stopping distance;
- speed adaptation;
- visibility and weather;
- vulnerable road users;
- distraction, fatigue and impairment;
- vehicle readiness and emergency response.

### Track C — International comparison (educational, not Saudi legal advice)
Allow a learner to compare how a concept is treated in selected jurisdictions. Every comparison card must show jurisdiction, source, version/date and a visible note that local rules differ.

## Original question design

Create new questions based on learning objectives, not copied wording or answer sets. Each item should have:
- unique ID, locale and jurisdiction;
- topic, subtopic, objective and difficulty;
- question type: single choice, multiple select, sign recognition, road-marking interpretation, scenario, hazard perception;
- original prompt, answer options, correct answer and detailed explanation;
- explanation for why each distractor is unsafe or incorrect;
- source references and the precise fact each source supports;
- legal status: binding local rule, official guidance, international framework, or general safety principle;
- content review status, reviewer, reviewed date, version and rights/licence metadata.

For each Saudi topic, author several distinct question forms: recall, interpretation, application, scenario and mixed mock-test variants. Do not merely paraphrase one question repeatedly.

## Adaptive preparation strategy

1. Diagnostic assessment across all topics.
2. Personal study plan weighted by the learner's weakest competencies.
3. Short lesson followed by retrieval practice.
4. Spaced repetition for signs, markings and rules.
5. Scenario and hazard-perception practice.
6. Mixed timed mock tests matching only the Saudi test format when that format is verified from an official source.
7. Readiness reporting by topic and confidence interval based on sufficient attempts; label it as an internal estimate, never an official pass prediction.
8. Error notebook that resurfaces missed concepts in new contexts.
9. Final readiness gate requiring broad topic coverage, stable performance across multiple fresh tests, and no critical safety misconception.

## Research and rights policy

- Prioritise statutes, official driver handbooks, official transport/DMV/police sites, and treaty bodies.
- Use commercial practice sites only to discover topic patterns, never as authoritative legal sources.
- Do not scrape, bulk-copy, redistribute or closely paraphrase protected question banks, handbooks, photos or diagrams without a licence.
- Record country, jurisdiction/state, vehicle class, test mode, language, source URL, source date, access date, and copyright/usage terms.
- Distinguish a country's legal rule from safe-practice advice and from an international convention.
- Do not assume a rule is shared merely because a sign looks similar.
- Do not claim exhaustive global coverage until a country-by-country source register and audit checklist are completed.

Each wave produces a jurisdiction profile, topic matrix, sign differences register, test format record and source/rights audit. The Saudi track remains the canonical learner path for the product's initial release.

## Release acceptance criteria

- Every published Saudi legal claim has a current primary source and review date.
- All Saudi sign/device records in the selected official corpus are accounted for, with coverage reports by class.
- Every international comparison clearly names its jurisdiction and cannot be mistaken for Saudi law.
- All questions are original or separately licensed, with provenance recorded.
- Fresh question sets are used for mock tests; answer leakage is prevented server-side.
- Adaptive practice demonstrably targets weaknesses without promising guaranteed success.
- A qualified Saudi traffic-safety reviewer signs off before public release.
